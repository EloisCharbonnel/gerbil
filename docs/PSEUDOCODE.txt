1. OVERVIEW
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  JPG photo (6000 x 4000 px, plain light background, ceramic + scale)
      |
      |-- reading, EXIF orientation, metadata (camera body, lens...)
      |
      |-- reduced copy (1500 px at most) for the analysis
      |       |-- background model (polynomial of degree 2, Lab space)
      |       |-- search for the scale: checkerboard -> single row -> ruler
      |       |       -> measurement at full resolution: pixels per cm (px/cm)
      |       |-- "object / background" map and first mask of the object
      |       |-- sorting: labels, handwriting, translucent supports, colour
      |       |   charts, scale
      |
      |-- refinement of the mask at full resolution (precise edges, anti-aliasing)
      |-- (--ai) AI second opinion: removes background the script kept
      |-- search for mounting putty (flagged, removed if --remove-putty)
      |
      |-- layout: ceramic at the top, straightened scale centred under it
      |-- PSD with 3 layers + layer masks   (NAME.psd)
      |-- TIFF ceramic alone                (NAME_outlined.tif)
      |-- TIFF ceramic + scale              (NAME_outlined_scale.tif)
      |-- control preview                   (to_control/NAME_to_control.jpg)
      |-- row of the CSV report             (outlined_report.csv)
      |
      '-- resolution stored: ppi = px/cm x 2.54  => real size 1:1
          in Illustrator or when printing.

  When something is doubtful (scale not found, not 5, 10 or 20 cm, possible
  mounting putty, ceramic cut by the edge...), all the files written get the
  "_to_be_verified" suffix.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. MATHEMATICAL AND IMAGE PROCESSING NOTIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 CIELAB colour space (Lab)
  The RGB (sRGB) pixels are converted to L*a*b*:
    L* = lightness (0 black -> 100 white),
    a* = green (-) / red (+) axis,
    b* = blue (-) / yellow (+) axis.
  Advantage: distances in Lab roughly match perceived colour differences.
  OpenCV gives L, a, b on 0-255; the script brings them back to the usual
  scale: L* = L x 100/255; a* = a - 128; b* = b - 128.
  Chroma (saturation): C* = sqrt(a*² + b*²).  Hue: h = atan2(b*, a*).

3.2 Distance to the background (weighted colour difference)
  d = sqrt( (0.5 x ΔL*)² + Δa*² + Δb*² )
  where Δ = colour of the pixel - colour of the background expected there.
  The 0.5 weight on lightness makes soft shadows (darker but of the same hue
  as the background) less visible than the ceramic (different hue).
  For unweighted measurements: ΔE = sqrt(ΔL*² + Δa*² + Δb*²).

3.3 2D polynomial regression by least squares (background model)
  The background of a photo is not uniform (lighting gradient). For each
  channel c (L*, a*, b*) we fit:
    c(x, y) = k1 + k2.y + k3.y² + k4.x + k5.x.y + k6.x²
  (x, y normalised between 0 and 1). The coefficients k minimise the sum of
  the squared differences over the background pixels: linear least squares
  (np.linalg.lstsq, solved by singular value decomposition).

3.4 Gaussian blur (cv2.GaussianBlur)
  Convolution by the Gaussian G(x, y) = 1/(2πσ²) . exp(-(x² + y²)/(2σ²)).
  Smooths the JPEG noise before the colour measurements. Uses:
    - 3x3 kernel (σ ≈ 0.8 px) before the object/background map;
    - 5x5 kernel (σ ≈ 1.1 px) before the mean colour measurements;
    - σ = 0.8 px to soften the edge of the mask (anti-aliasing);
    - σ = 1.2 px for the "high-pass" texture measurement (3.6);
    - σ = max(0.6; 0.08 x step) to smooth the profile of the graduations.
  (When only the kernel is given, OpenCV takes σ = 0.3 x ((n-1)/2 - 1) + 0.8.)

3.5 Other filters
  - 5x5 median filter (cv2.medianBlur): each pixel of the mask takes the
    median value of its neighbourhood; smooths the outline without rounding it.
  - 7x7 mean filter (cv2.blur, "box"): local mean.
  - Resampling: INTER_AREA (mean of the pixels covered) to reduce,
    bilinear (INTER_LINEAR) or bicubic (INTER_CUBIC) for rotations.

3.6 Texture (surface grain)
  high_pass = g - gaussian_blur(g, σ = 1.2)        (g = grey level)
  texture   = sqrt( mean_7x7(high_pass²) )         (local standard deviation)
  The high-pass removes slow variations (shading, curvature) and keeps the
  grain. Mounting putty or plexiglass is smooth; a ceramic is grainy.

3.7 Mathematical morphology (disc-shaped structuring element, function disk(r))
  - Erosion: a pixel stays "object" if the whole disc around it is object
    (thins, removes what is thinner than the disc).
  - Dilation: a pixel becomes "object" if the disc touches the object (thickens).
  - Opening = erosion then dilation: removes dust and filaments.
  - Closing = dilation then erosion: fills small cracks and holes.
  - Black top-hat ("black-hat") = closing(image) - image: brings out the dark
    strokes thinner than the disc (graduations, handwriting), whatever the
    lighting.

3.8 Hysteresis thresholding (function hysteresis)
  Two thresholds on the distance to the background: low = 8, high = 18.
  "Weak" areas (d > 8) are kept only if they contain at least one "strong"
  pixel (d > 18). The pale edges of a sherd are thus kept (connected to the
  sherd), but an isolated spot slightly different from the background (dust,
  shadow) is rejected.

3.9 Connected components (cv2.connectedComponentsWithStats)
  Labelling of the groups of touching pixels (8-connectivity: the 8
  neighbours; 4-connectivity: up/down/left/right). Gives for each group its
  area, its bounding rectangle and its centre. Used to separate sherds,
  labels, scale, holes of the mask...

3.10 Distance transform (cv2.distanceTransform)
  For each pixel of an object, distance to the nearest background pixel.
  Its maximum = radius of the largest inscribed disc = half-thickness of the
  object (used to recognise felt-pen writing, thinner than a sherd).
  Computed on the background, it gives the distance to the main object.

3.11 Geometry of outlines
  - Minimum-area rectangle (cv2.minAreaRect, "rotating calipers" method on
    the convex hull): orientation and size of a scale, of a graduation tick,
    of an elongated element.
  - Convex hull and convexity defects (cv2.convexHull,
    cv2.convexityDefects): hollows of the outline (to isolate a ball of putty).
  - Douglas-Peucker outline simplification (cv2.approxPolyDP, 1 px).
  - Sub-pixel polygon filling with anti-aliasing (cv2.fillPoly,
    coordinates x16, LINE_AA).

3.12 Affine transformations (function straighten)
  To straighten the scale, a crop rotated by an angle θ is extracted:
    [x_source]   [cos θ  -sin θ] [x_crop - cx]   [X_centre]
    [y_source] = [sin θ   cos θ] [y_crop - cy] + [Y_centre]
  The 2x3 matrix M sends the coordinates of the crop to the photo;
  cv2.warpAffine (WARP_INVERSE_MAP mode) fetches each pixel of the crop in the
  photo, with bilinear or bicubic interpolation.
  If the scale is straight (|θ| < 0.1°), the crop is an exact copy (odd sizes,
  integer centre). Turning the scale over = θ + 180°.

3.13 Sub-pixel measurements
  - Linear crossing (_crossing): position where a profile crosses 0.5 between
    two pixels: x = k - 1 + (0.5 - p[k-1]) / (p[k] - p[k-1]).
  - Parabolic peak (_peaks): parabola through 3 values a, b, c;
    peak at x = i + ½ (a - c) / (a - 2b + c).

3.14 Fitting a regular sequence (graduations of the ruler)
  The positions x_k of the ticks must satisfy x_k = a0 + step x k.
  - Common phase by circular mean (robust to spurious ticks):
      a0 = step x arg( mean( exp(2πi . x / step) ) ) / 2π
  - Then linear least-squares regression on (k, x_k), rejecting the points
    whose residual exceeds 0.2 step (5 iterations).
  - Quality: root mean square of the residuals (RMS) / step.

3.15 Robust statistics
  Median, percentiles (e.g. 98th percentile = "white" of the photo), median
  weighted by length (typical length of a square of the scale), standard
  deviation (alignment of the ends of the graduations).

3.16 Grouping ticks: union-find and spatial grid
  Each tick is an element; two compatible neighbouring ticks are "united"
  (union-find structure with path compression). A grid of fixed-size cells
  limits the search for neighbours to the adjacent cells (otherwise the cost
  would be quadratic).

3.17 Alpha compositing (Porter-Duff "over", function composite)
  To lay a layer (colour Cs, opacity As) over what already exists (Cd, Ad):
    A = As + Ad (1 - As)
    C = (Cs As + Cd Ad (1 - As)) / A
  On a white background ("matting"): C_white = C x A + 255 x (1 - A).

3.18 Lossless compression
  - PackBits (RLE, "run-length encoding") for the PSD: a run of 3 to 128
    identical bytes becomes (header 257 - n, value); otherwise "literal"
    packets of 1 to 128 bytes (header n - 1, bytes). Coded row by row, fully
    vectorised with NumPy.
  - LZW (or Deflate/ZIP) for the TIFF, with horizontal predictor: each pixel
    is stored as the difference with its left neighbour, which makes the data
    more compressible. No loss: unlike a new JPEG save, the image is not
    degraded.

3.19 Resolution and 1:1 scale
  ppi (pixels per inch) = px/cm x 2.54 (1 inch = 2.54 cm).
  Illustrator places an image at the size: width_cm = width_px / px/cm.
  Example: 703.86 px/cm -> 1787.8 ppi; an image of 3244 px is 4.61 cm wide.

3.20 Metadata
  - Embedded sRGB ICC profile (the one of the photo if it exists).
  - EXIF (copied into the PSD); XMP (PSD and TIFF): camera body, lens,
    focal length, date, aperture, shutter speed, ISO, serial number, and
    description of the measurement ("Scale detected (ruler): 5 cm,
    416.29 px/cm, ..."). Software name: "Gerbil <version>".

4. MAIN PROGRAM (main)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  READ the command-line options (argparse)
  files <- all the .jpg .jpeg .tif .tiff .png photos of the given folders
           (+ the photos given directly)
  IF no photo: print "No photo to process."; END
  IF --ai: check that onnxruntime is installed (otherwise: print the
      installation command; END); download the model if missing (once,
      MD5 check); print the model and the hardware used

  FOR each photo:
      output_folder <- -o option, otherwise <folder of the photo>/outlined_result
  CREATE each output_folder and its to_control subfolder

  plan_names: for each photo, the project of its folder (gerbil_project.json:
      fields, template, rule, common values, values of the photos):
      output name = template with the values (forbidden characters and
      spaces -> "_", separators of empty fields merged), else the name of
      the photo; same name twice in a result folder -> suffix _2, _3...
      (doubt); required fields empty -> doubt "missing information";
      rule (regular expression) not matched -> doubt
  IF several processes are asked for (-j, default = min(4, cores/2)):
      run process_photo(photo) in parallel (ProcessPoolExecutor;
      each process limits OpenCV to 2 threads)
  OTHERWISE: process_photo(photo) one after the other
  Each time a photo is finished: print a line
      [i/n] NAME: status (scale type, length, px/cm, duration) | notes

  remove_renamed: files written under a former output name (from the review
      data of the previous run) are removed, unless another photo uses it
  FOR each output_folder: write_report (CSV report, merged with the existing
      one; columns file, output_name, ..., then one column per field)
  Print the summary: number ok / to verify / failed, then print_summary: the
      reasons for doubt grouped by problem (PROBLEMS: scale not found, scale
      doubtful, no object kept, object cut by the photo edge, mounting putty,
      colour chart touching), each with the photos concerned
  IF --open: open the result folders in the Explorer

5. PROCESSING OF ONE PHOTO (process_photo)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  photo   <- load_photo(file)                                     [§6]
  f       <- min(1, 1500 / longest side)          (reduction factor)
  small   <- photo reduced by a factor f (INTER_AREA)
  bg      <- BackgroundModel( Lab( gaussian_blur_3x3(small) ) )   [§7]

  IF the photo has a manual scale (gerbil_scales.json next to the photos, or
      sherdcut_scales.json written before the renaming,
      written by "Set scale…" in the interface): scale <- manual_scale:
      px/cm = distance between the two points / length given (cm); scale
      layer = band of the given width around the segment, straightened;
      centimetre marks every cm; it prevails over everything else
  ELSE IF --no-scale: no scale
  OTHERWISE: scale <- detect_scale(photo, small, f, dark background?) [§8, §9]
             if not found: doubt "scale not detected"
  IF a scale is found: ppi <- px/cm x 2.54; possible measurement doubts
  OTHERWISE: ppi <- --default-ppi (300)

  IF --ai: ai <- ai_mask(photo)                                   [§10.3]
           (on failure: note "AI not used", the processing goes on without it)
  ceramic <- outline_ceramic(photo, small, f, bg, scale, ai)      [§10]
  IF a scale is found:
      putty <- find_putty(photo, ceramic, px/cm)                  [§11]
      IF putty is found:
          IF --remove-putty: remove it; doubt "mounting putty removed"
          OTHERWISE: flag it (red area); doubt "possible mounting putty"
  Notes: small isolated element discarded; translucent support removed;
         colour chart discarded; background removed by the AI
  IF a colour chart touches the ceramic: doubt "colour chart touching the ceramic"
  IF the cut-out touches the edge of the photo: doubt "ceramic cut by the photo edge"

  doubtful <- there is at least one doubt
  file names <- output_paths(NAME, doubtful)   (_to_be_verified suffix)

  IF a ceramic is found:
      size in cm = size of the cut-out / px/cm (for the report)
      layers, canvas <- layout(photo, ceramic, scale)             [§12]
      image_with_scale <- composite(visible layers)
      IF not --no-psd: write_psd(NAME.psd)                        [§13]
      IF scale: write_tiff(NAME_outlined_scale.tif, image_with_scale)
      write_tiff(NAME_outlined.tif, ceramic alone)                [§14]
      status <- "to verify" if doubtful, otherwise "ok"
  OTHERWISE status <- "failed"

  IF not --no-control: control_image(...)                         [§15]
      and write_review: the cut-out mask (photo size, PNG) and the scale
      outline, in outlined_result/gerbil_review, for the review window
  Corrections made in the review window (gerbil_edits next to the photos,
  read by edits_entry, right after outline_ceramic):
      corrected mask (0 / 255, photo size) -> cutout_from_mask: replaces
          the cut-out (edge softened as in §10.2: Gaussian blur 0.8 px);
          no putty search; note "cut-out corrected by hand"
      angle -> the object is turned at the layout (rotated_ceramic:
          rotation around the centre of its box, bicubic for the pixels,
          bilinear for the layer mask, one resampling; the scale and the
          hidden original photo are not turned); note "object turned by
          hand"; the sizes of the report are those of the turned object
  IF not --no-original-copy: copy_original(photo -> outlined_result)
  remove_stale: delete the other variant (with / without _to_be_verified)
                of the files just written
  ON ERROR: status "error", message in the notes
  RETURN the row of the report

6. READING THE PHOTO AND ITS METADATA (load_photo)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  RAW file (DNG, CR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, ORF, RW2, RAF, PEF, SRW, 3FR, IIQ):
      developed by LibRaw (rawpy.postprocess: white balance of the camera,
      sRGB, 8 bits, turned upright); EXIF read by Pillow from the TIFF
      structure of the file (copied at once, its sub-directories being read
      lazily), no ICC profile (sRGB)
  other files: open the file (Pillow); read the EXIF and the ICC profile;
      apply the EXIF orientation (photo taken in portrait -> turned upright)
  convert to 8-bit RGB
  note: make + model of the camera body, lens, focal length, date taken,
        aperture, shutter speed, ISO, serial number (for the report and the XMP)

  output_exif: copy of the EXIF updated for the output file
               (orientation = 1, resolution = ppi in inches, software;
                removal of the "MakerNote" and of the original dimensions)

7. BACKGROUND MODEL (BackgroundModel)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  lab <- Lab colours of the reduced photo (after a 3x3 blur)
  median <- median colour of the whole image (the background is the largest part)
  median_edges <- median colour of a 3 % band along the 4 edges
  IF d(median, median_edges) > 12: median <- median_edges
      (the object fills most of the frame: the global median would be its
       colour; the edges of the photo remain mostly background)
  background <- pixels whose distance d to the median < 12  (< 25 if too few)
  REPEAT 4 times:
      take 1 background pixel out of 5
      for L*, a*, b*: fit the polynomial of degree 2 in (x, y)
                      by least squares                            [§3.3]
      model <- value of the polynomial at each pixel
      background <- pixels whose d(pixel, model) < 6
  keep: the 6 coefficients per channel + the model at the small resolution
  dark background <- median lightness of the model L* < 35 (black velvet)

  evaluate(area): background colour expected over an area of the photo at full
  resolution: polynomial computed on a grid (1 point / 8 px) then enlarged by
  bilinear interpolation (the background varies slowly).


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. SQUARE SCALE: BLACK/WHITE/RED CHECKERBOARD OR SINGLE ROW (detect_scale)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

8.0 Dark background (black velvet): _scale_on_dark
  The black squares merge with the background: the search starts from the
  light squares.
  light <- min(R, G, B) > 0.55 x (99.5th percentile)
  thickness of the squares <- median of the short side of the solid,
      elongated light spots (L ≥ 2 S); if there are at least 3: set aside
      what is much thicker (opening, disc 0.75 x thickness: label stuck to
      the scale, light sherd); the opening is made on the light spots with
      their closed holes filled, so that a label is thick despite the writing
      on it (the black squares of the scale reach its edge: not filled)
  group the light spots by a dilation (disc 3): the white squares of a
  checkerboard only touch at their corners
  FOR the 6 largest groups, elongated (L ≥ 1.6 S):
      minimum-area rectangle around the light spots (it also covers the black
      squares of the corners), dilated by 2 px
      paint white everything outside the rectangle, and the 1 px border of
      the photo (what is copied beyond the edge becomes white)
      -> the scale looks as it does on a light background
      usual detection (§8.1 to 8.3) on these images, without the ruler and
      with L/S ≥ 2
  second pass, if this finds no scale of a usual length (5, 10 or 20 cm):
      groups of the light spots of BOTH masks (with and without the thick
      ones set aside: large white squares, not bands, count as "thick"), each
      rectangle lengthened 3 times along its long side (digits such as "10"
      or "cm" split the light squares, so a group may cover only part of the
      scale); second reading only (analyze_comb, §8.2b)
  kept: the first scale of a usual length; failing that, the first scale of
      the usual reading, or else the second reading with the most squares
      (doubtful length)

8.1 Locating at low resolution
  white <- 98th percentile of min(R, G, B) over the reduced photo
  scale_ink_classes:
      black: max(R, G, B) < 0.42 x white
      red:   R > 0.45 x white AND G < 0.42 x white AND B < 0.5 x white
             AND R - max(G, B) > 0.3 x white
  ink <- black OR red; closing (disc r = 3) to connect the squares
  (in a checkerboard, the squares touch at their corners)
  FOR each connected component of at least 120 px (and 60 px of ink):
      minimum-area rectangle -> angle θ, length L, width S
      reject if S < 3 px or L/S < 2.5 (not elongated enough)
      straightened crop (size 1.3 L x 4 S); analyze_pattern(crop)
      score = 10 x number_of_squares + L / image_width - 5 x irregularity
  IF no candidate: _single_row_groups (single row, separate squares)
      solid spots (filling > 55 % of their rectangle), of the same size
      (ratio < 1.6), spaced 1.2 to 3.5 times their side, aligned (distance
      to the line < 0.3 side) at an integer step; at least 3 -> candidate
  IF still no candidate: detect_ruler (ruler), then its second pass [§9.2b]
  IF still nothing: the same components read by analyze_comb      [§8.2b]
  OTHERWISE keep the candidate with the best score

8.2 analyze_pattern (crop where the scale is horizontal)
  white <- 98th percentile of min(R, G, B) in the centre of the crop
  ink <- black OR red (same rules)
  fr(y) <- proportion of ink in row y (between 12 % and 88 % of the width)
  printed face: rows around the maximum where fr > 0.25, tolerating a dip
                of a few rows (thin white line between the 2 rows of squares)
  top / bottom <- the two halves of the face (12 % margin)
  state(x) <- 1 if the top row is inked at x, 2 if the bottom one,
              3 if both, 0 if none  (threshold 40 % of the column)
  runs <- sequences of columns with the same state ("run-length")
  cleaning: runs that are too short (digits "5", "cm", arrow, graduations)
            are absorbed by their longest neighbour; threshold = 0.35 x
            typical length of a square (median weighted by length), 3 passes
  squares <- non-empty runs between the first and the last; n = their number
  scale cut by the photo edge (_inside: the crop goes beyond the photo, where
      the straightening copies the edge pixels): ink beyond the edge is
      ignored; a first / last square that starts at the edge is a cut square:
      counted (n_cut) but left out of the measurement; n = complete squares
  start / end of the scale: sub-pixel crossing of the profile at 0.5 [§3.13]
  length of a square = (end - start) / n
  REJECT if: square < 3 px; a square differs by more than 30 % from the
             mean; height/square outside [0.15; 3]; or, for a single row, if
             the light squares are not white (it would be painted bands on a
             ceramic)
  turn over <- the red is in the bottom row (the scale is upside down)
  RETURN face (y0, y1), start, end, n, square, boundaries, irregularity

8.2b analyze_comb: second reading (only when analyze_pattern finds nothing)
  Case of digits printed inside the squares ("5", "10" in a white square,
  "cm" in white on a red one): analyze_pattern takes such a square for an
  inked one. Here the squares are read by their BOUNDARIES:
  face, top / bottom rows: as in §8.2
  signed profile q(x) = ink(top)(x) - ink(bottom)(x)     (two rows: +1 / -1)
                 q(x) = ink(top)(x) + ink(bottom)(x) - 1 (single row)
      (Gaussian blur σ = 1 px; 0 beyond the photo edge)
      a digit lowers |q| inside its square but does not change its sign
  boundaries <- places where q changes sign between two plateaus |q| > 0.3
      (sub-pixel: linear crossing of 0)
  regular comb x_k = a0 + square x k fitted on the boundaries (circular mean
      then least squares, residuals > 0.15 square rejected, as for the ruler
      ticks §3.14); REJECT if RMS of the residuals > 8 % of the square
  squares: the longest run of squares between fitted boundaries whose mean q
      alternates in sign (|q| ≥ 0.3 to start, ≥ 0.12 inside: squares with
      digits); a boundary found on an object near the scale falls outside it
  end squares (two rows only): no sign change against the card or the
      background (both rows alike: q = 0); added while their sign continues
      the alternation (|q| > 0.15); a square cut by the photo edge: n_cut
  REJECT if fewer than 3 squares, square < 3 px, height/square outside
      [0.15; 3], or (single row) light squares not white, or (two rows) the
      row that is not inked is coloured: median chroma C* > 20 (CARD_CHROMA;
      the white of the cards measured 3 to 11, an orange sherd under written
      strokes 42: handwriting on a sherd is not a scale)
  never turned over: the red row is on top on some scales (DDN) and below on
      others (digits upright): the scale keeps its orientation in the photo
  The measurement uses all the boundaries (least squares): the ends of the
  scale (card edge, frame, blur) do not enter it.

8.3 Precise measurement at full resolution
  centre, θ, crop size <- candidate brought back to full resolution
  REPEAT at most 2 times:
      crop <- straighten(photo, centre, θ)                        [§3.12]
      res <- analyze_pattern(crop)
      IF res says "turn over" (red row below): θ <- θ + 180°; start again
  IF failure: try the ruler (§9, §9.2b), then analyze_comb (§8.2b), otherwise
      "scale spotted but unreadable"
  second reading: as long as the squares read reach an end of the crop (the
      component found at low resolution covered part of the scale), the crop
      is lengthened (squares read + 8 squares) around them and read again;
      note "squares read by their boundaries (second reading)"
  re-centre on the printed face; final crop with bicubic interpolation
  IF the digits of this final crop read upside down (§8.4): θ <- θ + 180°,
      final crop again (note "scale turned by reading its digits")
  length_px = end - start
  IF --scale-cm X: px/cm = length_px / X
  OTHERWISE: px/cm = length_px / n                (one square = 1 cm)
      length = n + n_cut cm; with a cut square, note "scale cut by the photo
      edge: measured on its n complete squares" (the measurement stays
      precise: the boundaries between complete squares are sharp)
      case of a single row ending with a white square (invisible on the white
      card): _card_side compares each end with the white of the inner squares
      and adds the square on the right side; the px/cm measurement is already
      right, only the outline of the scale changes
      IF the length is not 5, 10 or 20 cm (SCALE_LENGTHS): look for a ruler
          (§9, §9.2b); a ruler of 5 or 10 cm without doubt is kept instead
          (a number written on the sherd may look like a few squares);
          otherwise doubt
  IF irregularity > 15 %: doubt "irregular scale squares"
  outline of the scale <- rectangle of the printed face (flush with the
      squares, without the edge or the white border of the card); a cut
      square is kept up to the photo edge
  enlarged zone <- this rectangle + 0.35 square lengthwise and 1.3 face height
      (support, putty under the scale: kept out of the cut-out)


8.4 Orientation by the digits (digits_upside_down)
  The side of the red row differs between makes of scale (on top for the
  DDN scales, below for others, whose digits are then upright): the signs
  printed in the squares decide.
  templates <- "0123456789cm" drawn with 12 common Windows fonts (Arial,
      Calibri, Verdana, Tahoma, Segoe UI, Times; regular and bold), each
      sign cropped to its box, scaled into 24 x 24, zero mean, unit norm
  FOR each half-square (row) of the printed face (12 % margins):
      light half-square: sign = ink < 0.5 x white; dark or red: sign = light
      pixels > 0.7 x white; kept if 3 % to 45 % of the half-square
      FOR each connected component at least half as tall as the tallest
          (a character), 8 px at least:
          up   <- best correlation with the templates
          down <- best correlation of the component turned over (180°)
          IF |up - down| > 0.08 (GLYPH_MARGIN): vote += up - down
  upside down IF vote < 0; undecided IF no sign voted (or no font found):
      the red row decides, as before
  Applied to the final crop (re-centred, bicubic), once the red row is on
  top: on the first, coarser crop, 4 DDN scales were wrongly turned.
  A "m" turned over looks like a "w", a "5" like nothing: the templates
  only hold the signs of scales. Measured on 1,265 scales of known
  orientation: no error (undecided ones keep the red-row rule).
  Only for the usual reading (§8.2); the second reading (§8.2b) keeps the
  orientation of the photo.

9. RULER GRADUATED IN MILLIMETRES (detect_ruler)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

9.1 Locating (photo reduced to 3000 px)
  grey <- min(R, G, B)
  _thin_ticks: black-hat (disc r = 4) > 22 -> thin dark strokes
      keep the components of 5 to 2500 px, elongated (length ≥ 2.5 x width),
      thin (width ≤ 6 px); note centre, angle, length
  _tick_combs: group (union-find + grid, §3.16) two ticks if:
      angles within 10°; shift along the tick < 0.6 length;
      perpendicular distance < 1.6 length
    for each group of at least 8 ticks:
      axis of the ruler = perpendicular to the ticks
      step = median of the successive gaps along the axis
      regularity = share of the gaps within ± 25 % of the step (≥ 60 % needed)
    merge two groups aligned end to end (bent ruler, or graduations hidden by
      a support): angles within 4°, step within 5 %, lateral shift < 0.8 tick
      length, gap ≤ 8 steps
    keep the groups of at least 15 ticks, ranked by size x regularity

9.2 analyze_ruler (straightened crop, horizontal ruler)
  ink <- black-hat (disc ≈ ¼ step) > 22
  band of the graduations <- rows where the ink density ≥ 60 % of the maximum
      (looked for in the centre of the crop)
  profile(x) <- mean of the black-hat over the band
  _peaks: local maxima of the smoothed profile (Gaussian), above
      median + 0.35 x (max - median), at least 0.55 step apart, position
      refined by a parabola                                       [§3.13]
  fit x_k = a0 + step x k (circular mean then least squares, rejection of
      residuals > 0.2 step)                                       [§3.14]
  REJECT if fewer than 15 ticks or RMS of the residuals > 8 % of the step
  for each tick: top and bottom ends (vertical walk tolerating small gaps)
      -> length
  orientation: the graduations all start from the edge of the card; the side
      where the ends are aligned (smallest standard deviation) must be at the
      top, otherwise turn the ruler over (θ + 180°)
  centimetre ticks: among the 10 possible phases (k modulo 10), the one whose
      ticks are on average the longest (≥ 1.3 x median)
  n_cm = (last cm tick - first cm tick) / 10
  px/cm = 10 x step  (precision ≈ 0.1 % thanks to the ~50 intervals measured)

9.2a Crop lengthened (both passes)
  The group of ticks found at low resolution may cover only part of the
  ruler (object or shadow over it): as long as the first or last tick read
  lies within 2 steps of an end of the crop, the crop is redone around the
  ticks read, 24 steps longer than them, and read again (at most 3 times;
  kept only if more ticks are read). Otherwise the first or last
  centimetre tick is missed and the ruler is read as 2, 3 or 4 cm.

9.2b Second pass (detect_ruler robust), only when the first finds nothing
  Rulers with thick, long ticks hanging from the edge of the card (another
  make of ruler, or a closer shot):
  _tick_combs(long_ticks): grid cell sized on the LONGEST strokes (1.7 x)
      instead of the 95th percentile of all of them: on a sherd, hundreds of
      short strokes of its surface make the cell too small, and one tick
      missing breaks the chain of neighbours
  analyze_ruler(robust): band of the graduations <- rows where the density
      > 60 % of the 90th percentile of the densities (not of the maximum: the
      top of the ticks, darker along the card edge, is denser), crossing dips
      of up to 1/4 step (light line just under the card edge)
  A ruler bent or seen in perspective shows a regular (parabolic) drift of
  the ticks: RMS > 3 % of the step -> doubt "irregular graduations".

9.3 Validation, outline, zone
  doubts: n_cm neither 5 nor 10; RMS > 3 % of the step (deformed ruler);
          fewer than 80 % of the expected ticks (hidden graduations)
  ruler_outline (edges of the card):
      colour of the card = Lab median between the ticks
      d = Lab distance to the card; threshold = max(3; 2 x median in the band)
      top: go up as long as the row has the colour of the card (≤ 0.3 h)
      bottom: skip the space, find the band of the digits (ink), then go down
              to the edge of the card, at most 0.6 digit height lower (a label
              often touches the ruler)
      left / right: start just after the first / last tick and go on as long
              as at least half of the column is "card" (at most 12 mm)
  enlarged zone <- outline + 0.2 height on the sides, 0.5 above, 0.3 below

10. OUTLINING THE CERAMIC (outline_ceramic)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

10.1 First mask (photo reduced to 1500 px)
  d <- distance to the background (§3.2) of each pixel, with the model of §7
  object <- hysteresis(d, 8, 18)                                    [§3.8]
  object <- opening (disc 1) then closing (disc 2)                  [§3.7]
  object <- fill_holes(object):
      holes = background areas surrounded by the object (4-connectivity)
      a hole of ≥ 0.02 cm² whose mean distance to the background < 2.5 is a
      true opening (handle, perforation): it stays transparent;
      the others (gloss reflections, light concretions) are filled
  remove the rectangle of the scale (dilated by 2 px)
  translucent_supports (plexiglass supports):
      areas of the object where d < 15 (almost the background colour), after
      an opening (disc 0.25 mm), of at least 0.015 cm², with at least half of
      their perimeter on the background or an opening (they stick out of the
      object), and as light as the background (ΔL ≥ -8) or whiter than it
      (Δb ≥ +6)
      -> a reflection of the background on a black gloss is excluded: it runs
         along the ceramic, it is darker and of the same hue
      -> these areas are removed from the object
  remove_charts (colour charts), for each component ≥ 0.25 cm²:
      colour_chart:
          saturated pixels = chroma C* > 30 (§3.1)
          histogram of their hue h in 12 sectors of 30°
          sector present = at least 0.1 cm² of saturated pixels
          chart IF at least 5 sectors are present, with at least one warm
          (red, orange, yellow, magenta), one green and one blue
          (a ceramic, even glazed or painted, covers 2 neighbouring sectors at
          most: 1,763 photos tested; the charts 5 to 9)
      -> regular outline (solidity = area / area of the convex hull ≥ 0.95):
         chart alone, removed whole, red outline
      -> irregular outline: chart stuck to something else (sherd):
         chart_disc (round chart):
             inks of a hue foreign to ceramics: C* > 40 and h outside 30-90°
             (pinks, yellows, greens, blues)
             circle fitted by "algebraic" least squares:
               x² + y² + D x + E y + F = 0  ->  centre (-D/2, -E/2)
             2nd fit on the points of the coloured ring
             (|radius - median| < 35 %)
             valid IF the inks surround the centre (≥ 20 sectors of 10° out of
             36) and if the mask fills at least 60 % of the disc
             cutting radius = 99.5th percentile of the radii + 1.5 px
         -> circle found: the disc is removed (also at full resolution, after
            the filling of holes), then its cast shadow: pixels of the object
            within 1.6 radius, darker than the background (ΔL < -3) and of the
            colour of the darkened background:
              k = L / L_background;
              sqrt((a - k a_background)² + (b - k b_background)²) < 6
            connected to the edge of the disc; opening (disc 2) of what is left
         -> circle not found: chart kept, flagged (doubt)
  connected components of the object, kept if:
      - less than 50 % of their area in the enlarged zone of the scale, or
        an object lying close to the scale: it reaches at least 0.8 cm from
        the printed face (NEAR_SCALE_CM) and its mean distance to the
        background is at least 18 (NEAR_SCALE_D); note "object(s) lying
        close to the scale kept" (on 1,764 reference photos, what the zone
        discards reaching that far is a translucent support, mean distance
        13.2 at most; the card edge stays within 0.35 cm of the face)
      - not stuck to the edge of the photo with less than 30 % of the largest;
      - at least 0.25 cm²
      - on a dark background only, not a paper label (label_card): median
        L* ≥ 78 and filling ≥ 85 % of its minimum-area rectangle, and
        chroma ≤ 12, or b* < 0 and chroma ≤ 30 (bluish white: optical
        brighteners, from L* 72 when |a*| ≤ 5), or L* ≥ 90 and chroma ≤ 25
        (pale yellow note), or - whatever the colour - filling ≥ 95 % and
        L* ≥ 70 (very rectangular); red
        outline, note "paper label discarded". Before that, split_labels cuts
        each blob that is not a label at its narrow joins (opening, disc of
        2 % of its size) and removes a part (≥ 2 % of the blob) that is a label (label touching a
        sherd by a corner). If every object is a "label", they are kept and
        the photo flagged (a small pale square sherd alone)
  discard_labels:
      main object = the largest component (always kept)
      normal to the scale n; signed distance s = (point - centre) . n
      - discarded if entirely on the other side of the scale from the main
        object (labels placed under the ruler);
      - discarded if its inscribed radius (distance transform) < 0.85 mm
        (felt-pen writing, thread, printed line of a bag);
      - discarded if narrow (< 5 mm) and 12 times longer than wide (line);
      - discarded if small (< 8 % of the main object) and more than 8 mm from
        it (thick letter of a label); outlined in red on the preview if it is
        at least 4 % of the main object
  discard the components smaller than 4 % of the largest (crumbs)
  number of pieces = remaining components

10.2 Refinement at full resolution (useful area = object + 3 % margin)
  d <- distance to the background at full resolution (3x3 blur, background of §7)
  enlarged mask <- first mask enlarged (bilinear interpolation, > 0.5)
  r <- 1.5 / f + 2 px  (uncertainty due to the reduction)
  sure     <- erosion(enlarged mask, r) AND d ≥ 3.2
              (the background seen between two parts is never "sure")
  allowed  <- dilation(enlarged mask, r)
  mask     <- (d > 8 AND allowed) OR sure; keep what is connected to "sure"
  remove the scale; remove the translucent supports (d < 18)
  fill_holes (true openings ≥ 0.02 cm² kept open, as well as the holes that
             contained a removed support)
  dark background: edge_at_half_contrast - in the outer band of the mask
             (depth = max(8, longest side / 600) px), the pixels whose d is
             under half the d of the object nearby (Gaussian-weighted mean
             of d over the inner part, σ = 2 x depth) are removed, peeled
             from the outside only (4-connected to the background): the
             edge moves to the middle of the ramp (no dark outline)
  remove the discs of the colour charts again (a ring of shadow may surround
             them and get them filled)
  IF --ai: AI second opinion, subtraction only                    [§10.3]
  opening (disc 2); closing (disc 2); 5x5 median filter
  1 px erosion (avoids a thin line of background colour)
  alpha <- Gaussian blur σ = 0.8 px of the mask (softened edge, anti-aliasing)
  RETURN alpha, bounding rectangle, number of pieces, outlines of the
         discarded elements, of the removed supports, of the colour charts and
         of the areas removed by the AI (and their area)

10.3 AI second opinion (--ai option: ai_mask, then end of outline_ceramic)
  "Object / background" segmentation networks trained on millions of images
  (convolutional / transformer neural networks), in the ONNX format:
      isnet    : IS-Net, from the DIS project (Dichotomous Image Segmentation),
                 "isnet-general-use" model; fast (< 1 s per photo on the
                 graphics card)
      birefnet : BiRefNet "general"; finer on perforations, but ~1 GB: on the
                 processor (too large for DirectML with an 8 GB card; CUDA only)
  ai_mask (same pre-processing as rembg: masks identical to the pixel):
      image <- photo resized to 1024 x 1024 (Lanczos filter)
      image <- image / max(image); then (image - mean) / standard deviation
               isnet    : mean 0.5; standard deviation 1
               birefnet : means (0.485; 0.456; 0.406),
                          standard deviations (0.229; 0.224; 0.225) (ImageNet)
      p <- network(image)   (1024 x 1024 map)
      birefnet: p <- sigmoid(p) = 1 / (1 + e^-p)
      p <- (p - min) / (max - min); ai <- 255 x p
      if the graphics card fails (memory): done again on the processor
  Rule (in outline_ceramic, at full resolution):
      ai_object <- ai brought back to the useful area (affine transformation,
                   bilinear interpolation) > 127
      to_remove <- mask AND NOT dilation(ai_object, 3 px) AND d < 12
          = what the script keeps, that the AI sees as background, more than
            3 px from the edges it draws, and whose colour stays close to the
            background (background seen in a shadowed perforation, between two
            sherds, halo of a blurred edge)
      opening (disc 3); keep the areas ≥ 0.02 cm²
      mask <- mask AND NOT to_remove
  The AI never adds anything: when it is wrong, it is by keeping too much
  (label, support); these errors therefore have no effect.
  Note in the report: "AI (model) removed background: n area(s), x cm² (cyan
  outline in preview)".


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. MOUNTING PUTTY (find_putty)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  work at about 80 px/cm (reduction)
  texture <- local standard deviation of the high-pass (§3.6)
  FOR each outline of the object:
      simplify (Douglas-Peucker 1 px); convex hull
      hollows <- convexity defects deeper than 0.8 mm
      FOR each pair of hollows:
          cut the outline in two by the segment between the two hollows
          part <- the smallest piece (0.2 cm² to 45 % of the area)
          compare the part with the rest (areas eroded by 3 px):
              texture(part) < 0.5 x texture(rest)           (smoother)
              ΔE(mean colour of part, rest) > 6              (other colour)
              chroma(part) < 0.8 x chroma(rest)              (less coloured)
              L*(part) > max(45; L*(rest))                   (lighter)
          if all are true: candidate (the largest one is kept)
  IF --remove-putty:
      remove the part from the mask; clean the filaments around the cut
      (opening 0.4 mm) and the small leftovers (< 4 %)
  OTHERWISE: flag it (red area of the preview, _to_be_verified suffix)
  In every case the pixels stay in the PSD layer: only the layer mask
  changes, and the retouching stays possible with the brush.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. LAYOUT AND COMPOSITING (layout, composite)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  margin = 0.5 cm (--margin-cm); gap ceramic/scale = 0.5 cm (--gap-cm)
  (without a scale: 2 % of the photo)
  "recompose" layout (default):
      Scale layer <- straightened crop of the scale + anti-aliased rectangular
                     mask (§3.11)
      canvas: width = max(ceramic, scale) + 2 margins;
              height = margin + ceramic + gap + scale + margin
      ceramic centred at the top; scale centred under it
  "original" layout: scale left where it is; canvas = rectangle around
      ceramic + scale + margins
  Ceramic layer <- original pixels of the rectangle of the ceramic + 0.15 cm,
      with the alpha mask as layer mask
  Original photo layer <- the whole original photo, placed where it falls on
      the canvas ("photo -> canvas" offset): the scale stays where it was
      photographed, even when moved under the sherd; the layer goes beyond the
      canvas (negative coordinates are allowed in the PSD: Photoshop keeps these
      pixels); hidden (unless --no-original-layer)
  composite: "over" merging of the visible layers on a transparent
      background (§3.17)
  version without the scale: Ceramic layer alone, canvas = ceramic + 2 margins

13. WRITING THE PSD (write_psd) - Adobe Photoshop format, written byte by byte
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  header: "8BPS", version 1, 4 channels (RGB + alpha), height, width,
          8 bits, RGB mode
  resources:
      0x03ED resolution = ppi (16.16 fixed-point number), shown in cm
      0x0421 version information (composite image present)
      0x040F ICC profile (sRGB)
      0x0422 EXIF
      0x0424 XMP (description of the measurement + shooting information)
  layers (from bottom to top: Original photo, Scale, Ceramic):
      rectangle (signed coordinates: may go beyond the canvas), channels
      (-1 full opacity, 0 R, 1 G, 2 B, -2 layer mask), "normal" mode, opacity
      255, visible / hidden, mask data (20 bytes), blending ranges (40 bytes),
      name (Pascal string + "luni" Unicode block), "lyid" identifier
      data of each channel: PackBits row by row (§3.18)
  negative number of layers = the 4th channel of the composite image is the
      transparency
  composite image: colours "matted" on white + alpha channel, PackBits
  Checked with the independent psd-tools library: file read without error,
  rendering of the layers identical to the composite image.

17. KNOWN LIMITS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - The measurement is right only if the scale is in the plane of the object.
  - The background must be plain and different from the ceramic. A light
    background is the intended situation. On black velvet the scale is found
    from its light squares (§8.0): 41 of 42 photos of a 2025 sample (5, 10
    and 20 cm checkerboards with digits in the squares); on 15 old photos of
    448 px, 12 of the 14 that show a scale, plus one of doubtful length (scale
    cut by the edge); the last one (squares of 13 px in three rows) needs the
    manual scale. Enlarging the photo before the search was tried: it found
    nothing more and gave a wrong reading.
  - Scale forms supported: black/white/red checkerboard (one or two rows,
    digits inside the squares allowed, 5, 10 or 20 cm), ruler graduated in
    millimetres. Another form is most often flagged _to_be_verified.
  - Opaque white or cream supports (putty, foam) lodged in a hollow or behind
    a handle look too much like a light ceramic: they are not recognised and
    are retouched with the brush on the mask of the Ceramic layer.
  - Mounting putty is only flagged by default: a light, smooth vessel base
    looks too much like it to remove it straight away.
  - The --ai option only opens background whose colour is close to the
    background: it fixes neither opaque supports nor mounting putty. Its
    results depend on the model used (noted in the report).

18. INTERFACE AND .EXE VERSION
  ReviewWindow ("Review…"): photos of the last results (doubtful first);
      display = photo, background dimmed, outline of the mask (morphological
      edge), scale outline; tools: brushes (cv2.line / cv2.circle on the
      full-size mask) or lasso (cv2.fillPoly), removal of a piece (flood fill
      from the click), undo (saved patches); the photo of the view is cached
      and the redraws are limited to one every 30 ms while painting; orientation: line A-B -> angle φ such
      that the line becomes horizontal with the centre of the object below
      it (rim on top), or quarter turns; Accept: mask PNG + {"mask", "angle"}

19. PLATES (gerbil_plates.py)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  collect(results folder): every NAME_outlined.tif (and _to_be_verified if
      wanted) with a resolution; the object = bounding box of its alpha > 0
      (the transparent margin left out; white background: the whole image);
      real size (cm) = pixels of that box / dpi x 2.54; the row
      of outlined_report.csv of the same output name gives the fields
  sort: by group, then by the order field, then by name (natural order:
      2 before 10)
  layout (rows): paper size of an object = real size x 10 / ratio (mm);
      cell = max(object width, caption width measured with the font);
      a row is closed when the next cell does not fit in the page width,
      a page when the next row does not fit above the scale bar; a new
      group closes the row (or the page); rows centred, images aligned on
      their bottom edge, caption 1.25 x its size under them
  Drawing: list of shapes in mm (rect, poly, text, image) by layer, written
      either as SVG (width/height in mm, viewBox in mm, layers as
      <g inkscape:groupmode="layer">, images as PNG data URIs at the plate
      dpi) or drawn with Pillow for the preview (same coordinates x px/mm,
      text anchored on its baseline like SVG): both show the same thing
  draw_bar (in the manner of the QGIS scale bar):
      length L (real cm; auto = largest of 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50 cm whose paper
      length 10 L / ratio fits in a third of the width, at most 10 cm at 1:1),
      n segments (auto: one per cm up to 10 cm, otherwise 4 or 5), first
      segment cut in k parts; cells alternate colours (the subdivisions among
      themselves, the segments keep the colours they have without them)
      styles: single box, double box (two rows, colours crossed), hollow
      (outline + half-height band), line ticks up / down / middle (full ticks
      at segments, half ticks at subdivisions), stepped line
      numbers: real values (x10 when the unit is mm) at the ends or at every
      segment, centred on their tick, the unit after the last one; ratio
      "1:r" after the bar if wanted
  title (optional): {page} {pages} {group} replaced, left / centred / right
      at the top margin; file names: template (plate_{page} by default,
      page on 2 digits), characters not allowed by Windows replaced by "_",
      the number added when {page} is missing, duplicates numbered
  scales: object_ratio = the scale chosen for the object, else the plate
      scale, else (fit_large) the first of 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10,
      15, 20 at which it fits the page; layout() lays out each scale
      separately (layout_one) - one scale per plate, its own bar; {scale}
  by material: material_ratio = first rule "word=reduction" whose word is
      in the material column (1/3 -> 1:3, 2/3 -> 1:1.5); then fit_large
  mix_scales: one layout with each object at its own scale, "(1:r)" added
      to the caption, one bar per scale stacked at the bottom (named 1:r)
  drawings: attach_drawings - files .svg/.ai/.pdf/.eps of the folder; name
      without Gerbil's suffixes = output name ({name}), or fields read with
      the template and compared with the object's (letters and digits only,
      leading zeros ignored); .ai/.pdf/.eps -> SVG by Inkscape (cached);
      size from the root width/height (mm, cm, in, pt, pc, px at 96/in) x
      the drawing scale; placed beside the photo (gap / 2), bottom-aligned;
      written as a nested <svg> (viewBox kept, ids prefixed) in the layer
      "Drawings"; preview: PNG by Inkscape (cached)
  draw_measures (option "measurements"): width and height of the placed box
      (real cm, 1 decimal) as dimension lines 2.5 mm above and left of the
      object, end ticks 1 mm, the height written along its line (rotated
      -90°); layer "Measurements" written with style="display:none" (hidden
      in Illustrator / Inkscape), drawn in the preview only on demand

20. SITE PROFILES AND LEARNED SCALES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  profile: --profile FILE, else gerbil_profile.json next to the photo
      {"background": auto|light|dark, "learned_first": bool, "scales": [model],
       "settings": options of the interface (ignored by the engine)}
      background light / dark: replaces the decision of BackgroundModel
  learn_scale(photo, two points, length, band width): manual_scale crop
      (straightened band between the points) resized to 2400 px long; kept
      as PNG (base64) with its px/cm
  detect_learned_scale(photo, models): photo reduced to 3000 px; SIFT
      points (20,000 on the photo, 8,000 on each model); matches: ratio test
      0.8; RANSAC similarity (estimateAffinePartial2D, 3 px); refused with
      fewer than 8 inliers or an absurd size (under 5 % or over 150 % of the
      photo); fine: the photo warped into the model frame, ECC affine
      (findTransformECC, Gaussian 5x5), refused under correlation 0.80;
      best model = highest correlation; its two ends -> manual_scale (kind
      "learned"); correlation under 0.90: doubt ("another copy of the
      scale?")
  order: usual detection first, the learned scales when it fails or has a
      doubt; learned_first: the learned scales first
  measured: Qusur 2025, 10 cm scales learned from one photo each: same
      physical scale 0.02-0.05 % from the checkerboard reading; one more
      photo read (37 -> 38 ok), all the others identical

22. ONE-CLICK CUT-OUT (review window)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  model: MobileSAM (MIT), ONNX export by Acly (Hugging Face): image encoder
      (28 MB) + single-mask SAM decoder (16.5 MB), downloaded once into
      models/ and checked with their SHA-256
  ClickCutout.set_image: photo reduced to 1024 px (longest side), RGB 0-255
      (normalisation and padding inside the model) -> embedding 256x64x64
  ClickCutout.mask: points (x, y) x 1024 / longest side, label 1 (keep), plus
      the padding point (0, 0) label -1; no mask input; output at the size of
      the photo, logits > 0
  click_refine: band = max(6, longest side / 400) px around the edge of the
      SAM mask; the computed cut-out pieces lying mostly inside the mask
      (eroded by the band) give the edge where they cover over 80 % of it;
      otherwise the SAM mask
  review window: tool "One click on the object": the photo is encoded in a
      thread when the tool is chosen; first click: the cut-out is replaced by
      the object; next clicks add objects; Shift + click removes one; each
      click is undoable
